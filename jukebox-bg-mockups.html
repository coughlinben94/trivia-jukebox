Jukebox live-screen background — 4 motion concepts

Each stage is a real animation, not a still. Record + title/artist text sit on top with a scrim, same as the live app, so you can judge legibility. "Next Song" swaps the two source colors and runs the crossfade transition. "Replay Entrance" resets to black and runs the first-song arrival.

1 — Tide
2 — Duel
3 — Ecosystem
4 — Aurora

Tide

A single fluid boundary between the two colors, warped by slow domain noise so it never reads as a circle or a straight line. The boundary itself creeps forward and pulls back over the course of the song — like a tide line, not a wobble in place.

Entrance: color floods in from a random point on the edge of the screen and spreads across, like water spilled and finding the corners — no reveal mask, the liquid itself is the wipe.

Transition: outgoing frame freezes as a single image, new field fades up underneath it and takes over.

closest to current renderer, but boundary now travels + entrance is literal liquid spread

Duel

Two independent color fields, each with its own slow-random "confidence" level. When one color's confidence rises it visibly pushes across the midline into the other's territory; when it falls it retreats. Both levels drift independently and never repeat in sync — reads as two things pushing and yielding, not one boundary wobbling.

Entrance: both fields start collapsed to zero at opposite edges and grow inward, meeting in the middle for the first time as the song starts.

Transition: same freeze-then-fade crossfade as concept 1.

most literal read on "battling / dancing"

Ecosystem

No single boundary at all — a field of soft drifting motes in both colors, moved by a shared slow flow field so they clump and disperse like plankton in water. The population balance between the two colors drifts slowly over the song (60/40 one minute, 30/70 the next) so the overall cast of the screen visibly shifts, not just the texture.

Entrance: motes spawn from one edge and drift in, density ramping from empty to full.

Transition: existing motes recolor gradually toward the new pair while old motes fade and new ones spawn — a population turnover rather than a hard cut.

most literal read on "it's an ecosystem"

Aurora

Soft diagonal ribbons of the two colors drift across the frame at slightly different speeds and widths, crossing and overlapping rather than meeting at one edge. Where they overlap you get a third blended tone that shifts as the ribbons slide past each other. Motion has a clear direction (diagonal drift) rather than a stationary pulse, which reads as more "alive" than a fixed boundary.

Entrance: ribbons stream in from one corner, like the screen is being painted on.

Transition: freeze-then-fade, same as the others.

most structurally different from the current renderer
